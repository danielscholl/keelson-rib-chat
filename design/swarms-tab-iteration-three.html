A Swarms tab that shows its work

What the tab shows a human in its third pass: a launcher that stays out of the way, a history that reads by outcome, a board that says who did what at what cost, and a record page the rib draws for every swarm. Every part renders on the canvas Keelson 0.112 already ships.

Today the tab opens on the launcher whenever no swarm is live, so an operator who comes back to read a result scrolls past the form to reach it. Ended swarms are a list of identical done rows keyed by the task that was asked, not by what came of it; a rerun is a twin of the row above it, and the time column switches from a clock to a date part way down. What a swarm did, meaning who worked when, what each agent spent, and which run made which branch and pull request, sits on the swarm summary and never reaches the tab.

This pass keeps the routing, the limits and the three places (the board, the run drawer, ClickClack) as they are and changes what the rib composes. The launcher folds to one line once a swarm exists and opens as a two-field quick start, with adjust revealing the rest. Ended rows lead with the outcome and group by day. The board carries one outcome card, run rows with the branch and every pull request, a spend strip by agent, and an activity feed that names its actors. A record page, drawn by the rib as an html view, shows the swarm's timeline: a lane per agent, turns as spans, and gates, questions and operator posts as marks. Every element is canvas vocabulary Keelson 0.112 renders today; appendix B lists the rib-side changes.

No Keelson change and no version bump. The tab still does not mirror the channel, answer approvals, or run tools from a page: an action posted from inside an html view can raise a toast and nothing else, so every verb stays on the board. Spend stays in tokens, since the summary carries no prices. Search and filters stay out, because a frame is shared by every viewer. Cancelling one run from the tab is not part of this change.

Colors identify who owns each part

Rib · composes every frame from the swarm summary Host · renders the vocabulary, the drawer, forms and toasts Operator · reads, answers, messages, stops ClickClack · holds the conversation and the transcript Dashed · a section or seat that appears only when it has content

The mockups draw agents in the tab's own identity tones, the way the host draws them on the bench, and reproduce the host's board parts. Ids, tasks, handles and figures are example values.

The index at rest

The launch board carries the host's fold hint, defaultCollapsed, set whenever the store holds a swarm, so the region is open on an empty tab and folds after the first launch. The host folds it once when the hint turns on and keeps the operator's own fold after that; a store emptied by a reset opens it again. The folded head's byline names the launch a click would start, so an operator who never opens the form knows what Start does.

An ended row leads with what came of the swarm: the report's title when the lead published one, else the first sentence of the conclusion cut at 60 characters, else the cause (Stopped by you, Out of turns at 20, Failed: ClickClack unreachable). The task follows after · for:, cut so the line stays under 90 characters and on one line at the tab's usual width. A done row carries a muted check; the other four lifecycles carry a toned chip, so emphasis is spent on the exceptions. A rerun carries ↻ before its text and keeps its own row, because reruns exist to compare outcomes. Rows group under the day they ended: Today, Yesterday, then the weekday and date, and within a day the time is always a clock. The eight newest rows stay on the tab; the history drawer shows every ended swarm in the same day groups.

The running card is unchanged from the second pass. The launcher and the footer fold to their heads; the ended rows carry the outcome first and the task second.

The launcher

The form keeps every field it has and shows three of them at rest: the task, the project, and a Setup segment with two choices, defaults and adjust. The size, power and model override fields carry showWhen on the segment with equals: "adjust", a host primitive since 0.112, so they appear only when the operator asks for them. Agents may and workflows keep the rule they have today and appear once a project is chosen: a field takes one showWhen, and those two mean nothing without a project. A hidden field is never dispatched, so a launch on defaults carries no size or model in its payload and the rib fills in medium, balanced and the power's model. The defaults are written on the segment itself and in the folded byline, and the size segments keep their agent and turn counts when they appear.

The host keeps a form's values between frames, so choosing adjust, changing the size, then choosing defaults again launches on defaults while the typed task survives; a line under the segment says so. Prepare in chat stays as the second button and says what it is for. The link refusal is unchanged: a task that names a URL or an issue number with no context item is refused inline before a channel exists.

With adjust chosen, size, power and model override appear under the segment in the order they have today. Choosing a project adds agents may and workflows beside it.

The swarm board

The board keeps its two orders, live and ended, and its one actions section. What changes is how the outcome, the runs, the bench and the activity are composed. The report card and the conclusion card become one outcome card: the report's title is the card's title, the conclusion is its prose, the footnote names the author, the time, the length and the report's size, Open the report and Read the conclusion sit on the card, the prose carries the host's copy button, and a field links the swarm's channel in ClickClack. When the lead published no report the card is titled Conclusion; when the swarm did not conclude the cause card stays as it is.

The ended board's actions row carries Run again as today and gains Open the record. The channel link moves up to the outcome card, since an action button carries no link, and stays in About. The live board's row carries Message the lead, Open the record and Stop at the far end.

Run rows keep the evidence bar and the Open run verb and say more in their text: the branch the run checked out, every pull request rather than the first, how long it took, and for a failed or cancelled run the first 90 characters of why. The full error and the CI detail go to the reading pane, because a row that carries a verb cannot also carry a disclosure. Bench cards gain a footnote with the agent's last event and its time. A Spend section of inline bars follows the bench with one bar per agent, fresh tokens against the swarm's fresh total, the handle as the label and no tone, since spend is a magnitude and not a judgment. Activity rows name their actor: a chip in the agent's identity tone, or you for the operator, then the event without the handle repeated in the text. A turn is one row, written when it ends, with its outcome, duration and the messages it answered; its start goes to the op's progress log only.

On the real board the task and context rows, the twelve newest events and the About rows follow the Spend section, as today.

The swarm record

The record is a page the rib draws for each swarm and registers as an html view, rib:chat:record:<id>, beside the swarm's board, doc and report keys. It is composed from the same summary the board is and released with the swarm's other keys. Each swarm's record is registered with its board and reading pane, because the host has to know a key holds an html page before an action opens it. A live record recomposes when a turn, an agent, a run, a gate, the report or the conclusion changes, at most every five seconds, because the host reloads a frame whenever its document changes; an ended one is composed once. So an operator can watch the shape of the work form and read it back later. The host pushes the theme into the frame and sizes it to its content, and the page paints from the kit's tokens, so it matches the tab in both themes.

The page is a drawing, not a control surface. An action posted from inside an html frame produces a toast and no effect, and each new frame reloads the document, so the record carries no buttons and holds no state; the board keeps every verb. It also does not carry the lead's report or the transcript. The report has its own page, and the transcript is ClickClack's.

The timeline is the page's first figure. One lane per agent in the order they first took a turn, the operator's lane at the top, and one lane per run at the bottom. A turn is a span from its start to its end in the agent's identity tone, hatched when it timed out or errored. Marks sit on the lanes for what changed the swarm's course: a spawn, a gate opening and its answer, a question to the operator, an operator post, the report, the conclusion, a run's verification. The axis is minutes since the swarm started. While the swarm is live the axis runs a quarter past the elapsed time, at least ten minutes and at most to the wall clock limit, with a marker at now and the end time named at the right edge; a four-hour axis would squeeze the first half hour into a sliver. After the swarm ends the axis ends where it did.

Turn spans come from the summary's turn list; the marks come from activity entries that carry a kind and an actor, the agents' join times and the runs' gate history. The lead's fourth turn is twelve seconds wide: it answered the gate and stopped.

Validation

Every board and the record are pure functions of the swarm summary, so the builders are tested against fixture summaries, one per state the design names: a running swarm with no request; each request kind alone; an ended swarm with a report; one with a conclusion and no report; one stopped with one of three runs verified; one out of turns; one that failed before boot; a rerun of an ended swarm; swarms that ended today, yesterday and last week; and a swarm at the start bounds, 12 agents and 200 turns, for the record's size. Each frame passes expectView(key, kind), the validator the host applies before it broadcasts. The frame budgets stay: the index under 48 KB with six live swarms, the board under 48 KB, the doc pane and the record under 128 KB.

The launcher is tested at the action: a launch on defaults dispatches no size, power or model and the swarm records medium and balanced; a launch with adjust and a size sends the size and the swarm records it; a task that names a link with no context is refused before a channel exists. The fold is tested on the launch board: the hint is off when the store is empty and on once it holds a swarm.

The record is rendered once from the caps fixture with headless Chrome in both themes, and the screenshots go in the pull request beside the index and the board at 1200 and 640 pixels. The live smoke test asserts that a scripted swarm registers its record view at start, that the view recomposes through running and done, and that the ended row's text leads with the conclusion's first sentence. The record's text is escaped and the page carries no script, which a fixture with markup in its task, handles and errors checks.

The chat docs corpus in src/docs.ts restates the tab's layout in prose and changes with it, and deferred.md drops the Keelson surface it still lists as not done.

C · Alternatives not chosen

AlternativeWhy not
The host's graph view for who woke whomIts nodes take a label and a plain chip, no tone and no link, so the agents would lose the identity colors the bench gives them. The record draws the same graph with them.
The host's log kind for the activity logIt renders verbatim text with no links, and the reading pane's log links each ask to its thread.
A table section for runsCells carry links, not actions, so the rows would lose Open run. Rows keep the evidence bar and the verb.
Search or a filter on the history drawerA frame is shared by every viewer, so a filter would be shared state. Day groups keep thirty rows scannable.
Cards for ended swarmsEight rows fit above the fold; eight cards do not.
Collapsing reruns into one rowReruns exist to compare outcomes; a collapsed row hides the comparison.
The timeline as a chart sectionA chart plots values over time and caps at six series; a timeline is spans on lanes.
A cost figure in currencyThe summary carries no prices, and a rate table kept in the rib goes stale with every model change.
Verbs on the record pageAn action from inside an html frame produces a toast and no effect; a button that only toasts is a dead end.
A one-line inline launcher instead of the foldAn action form renders as a block. The folded head gives the same one-line rest state without a new primitive.
A router wakes list counted at dispatchRun notes and nudges wake the lead without passing the router, so a count there misses them. The turn runner sees every cause, so each span records its own.
Folding a turn's start entry into its end in placeThe activity list is oldest first, and an in-place update would put a finished turn at its start time and break the newest-last reading the index relies on.
Registering an ended swarm's record when it is first openedThe host has to know a key holds html before an action opens it; a key registered by the opening action is drawn as an unknown view.
A record composed on every changeThe host reloads an html frame whenever its document changes, so a busy swarm's record would reload several times a second and lose its scroll.
Mirror the channel, embed ClickClack, answer approvals from the tabDecided against in the first design; nothing here changes those reasons.
